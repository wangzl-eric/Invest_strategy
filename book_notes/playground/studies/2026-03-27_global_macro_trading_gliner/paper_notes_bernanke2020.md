# Paper Notes: "The Effects of Quantitative Easing on Financial Conditions" — Bernanke (2020)

**Citation:** Bernanke, B. (2020). The New Tools of Monetary Policy. *Brookings Papers on Economic Activity*, Spring 2020.
**Scores:** C:5 / R:4 / A:4

---

## Core Thesis

Quantitative easing (QE) and forward guidance are effective monetary policy tools that work primarily through portfolio balance effects and expectations channels — not money printing. Both tools eased financial conditions significantly during 2008–2015 and have become permanent parts of the CB toolkit.

---

## Key Findings

- **Portfolio balance channel:** When the Fed buys long-term assets, it forces investors into riskier assets (equities, credit, EM), compressing term premia and risk premia across the board.
- **Signaling channel:** QE announcements commit the Fed to keeping rates low, shifting rate expectations — often the more powerful channel.
- **Term premium compression:** QE reduced the 10-year term premium by ~100bps at peak, directly reducing long-term borrowing costs beyond what short rate expectations justified.
- **Forward guidance:** Explicit calendar or threshold-based commitments reduce rate uncertainty and stimulate investment via lower long-term rates.
- **Limitations:** QE loses effectiveness near the ZLB when portfolio substitution is limited (all assets near zero). Negative rate side effects (bank profitability) constrain how deep rates can go.
- **Exit risk:** Balance sheet normalization (QT) is the reverse — reduces portfolio balance support, widens term premia, tightens financial conditions.

## Connection to Gliner (GMT)

- **Ch11 (central banks):** Provides the authoritative framework for QE transmission — the same framework Gliner describes qualitatively.
- **Ch9 (fixed income):** Term premium compression and its reversal (QT) are the dominant drivers of long-end yields in the post-GFC era.
- **Ch4 (building blocks):** QE suppresses risk premia across all asset classes simultaneously — the key cross-asset macro theme of 2009–2021.

## Implementation Notes

- **QE regime signal:** Track Fed balance sheet size (FRED: WALCL). Rapid expansion = risk-on tailwind; contraction = headwind.
- **Term premium:** Adrian-Crump-Moench (ACM) term premium (NY Fed) is the cleanest measure. Negative term premium = QE distortion present.
- **Cross-asset:** During QE, carry strategies outperform; during QT, they underperform as risk premia normalize.
