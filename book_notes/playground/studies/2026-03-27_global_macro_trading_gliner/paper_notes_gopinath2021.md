# Paper Notes: "Banking, Trade, and the Making of a Dominant Currency" — Gopinath & Stein (2021)

**Citation:** Gopinath, G., Stein, J. (2021). Banking, Trade, and the Making of a Dominant Currency. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 136(2), 783–830.
**DOI:** 10.1093/qje/qjab002
**Scores:** C:5 / R:5 / A:4

---

## Core Thesis

The USD's dominance as a global reserve and trade-invoicing currency is self-reinforcing via a complementarity between trade invoicing and bank dollar funding. Countries that invoice trade in USD also hold more dollar assets, deepening dollar liquidity — which further entrenches invoicing in dollars. This creates a stable equilibrium that is very difficult to displace.

---

## Key Findings

- **Dominant currency paradigm:** ~50% of global trade is invoiced in USD regardless of whether the US is a counterparty. EUR invoices ~30% of non-euro-area trade.
- **Self-reinforcing mechanism:** USD invoicing → dollar asset demand by trading firms → dollar bank funding → dollar liquidity → lower dollar borrowing costs → more USD invoicing.
- **Safe asset demand:** Dollar dominance is linked to the global demand for safe USD assets (Treasuries). Countries accumulate reserves in dollars to self-insure.
- **Exchange rate pass-through:** Because trade is dollar-invoiced, a USD appreciation raises import costs globally — a contractionary shock that tightens financial conditions everywhere.
- **Implications for monetary policy:** Fed tightening has outsized global spillovers because dollar appreciation raises borrowing costs for all dollar-indebted entities worldwide.

## Connection to Gliner (GMT)

- **Ch7 (FX):** Explains structurally why DXY is not just another currency pair — USD moves affect global financial conditions, not just bilateral trade.
- **Ch11 (central banks):** Fed policy has global transmission via dollar dominance. This is why Fed rate hikes matter for EM FX more than any local CB decision.
- **Cross-asset:** Dollar strength = tighter global financial conditions = headwind for risk assets everywhere.

---

## Implementation Notes

- **USD as global risk-off indicator:** DXY strength signals tightening global liquidity — reduce risk exposure across EM equities, EM debt, commodities.
- **Fed divergence trades:** When Fed tightens relative to ECB/BOJ, dollar dominance amplifies the FX move beyond what rate differentials alone predict.
- **EM carry risk:** EM carry strategies face asymmetric tail risk when USD strengthens — dollar dominance means EM CBs must defend currencies even at cost to growth.
