# Paper Notes: "The Trilemma in History" — Obstfeld, Shambaugh, Taylor (2005)

**Citation:** Obstfeld, M., Shambaugh, J., Taylor, A. (2005). The Trilemma in History: Tradeoffs among Exchange Rates, Monetary Policies, and Capital Mobility. *Review of Economics and Statistics*, 87(3), 423–438.
**DOI:** 10.1162/0034653054638340
**Scores:** C:5 / R:5 / A:4

---

## Core Thesis

The impossible trinity — a country cannot simultaneously maintain a fixed exchange rate, free capital flows, and independent monetary policy — is empirically validated across 130+ years and 21 countries. The trilemma constrains central bank choices and directly determines the transmission of monetary policy into FX and interest rates.

---

## Key Findings

- **Trilemma confirmed empirically:** Countries with fixed exchange rates and open capital accounts have interest rates that closely track the base country (loss of monetary independence). Countries with floating rates retain independence.
- **Historical regimes map cleanly:** Gold standard (fixed + open capital = no independence), Bretton Woods (fixed + closed capital = some independence), post-1973 float (independent monetary policy).
- **Interest rate pass-through:** Under fixed exchange rates with open capital, ~85% of base-country rate changes pass through to the pegging country. Under floating rates, pass-through drops to ~30–40%.
- **Capital mobility matters:** The trilemma only binds when capital is mobile. Closed capital accounts give countries more freedom regardless of exchange rate regime.
- **Policy implication:** A central bank's choice of exchange rate regime determines how much autonomy it has over domestic rates — and therefore how macro shocks transmit across borders.

---

## Methodology

- Sample: 1870–1999, 21 countries, annual data.
- Classifies countries by exchange rate regime (fixed/floating) and capital mobility (open/closed).
- Measures interest rate co-movement with base country as proxy for monetary independence.
- Difference-in-differences framework across regime types.

## Connection to Gliner (GMT)

- **Ch11 (central banks):** The trilemma is the foundational framework Gliner uses to explain why central banks face constrained choices and how policy divergence drives FX moves.
- **Ch7 (FX):** Exchange rate regime choice directly determines whether interest rate differentials (carry) are stable or volatile.
- **Cross-asset:** When a country abandons a peg (e.g., SNB 2015, GBP 1992), the trilemma predicts the direction and magnitude of the FX and rates shock.

## Implementation Notes

- **Regime classification:** IMF publishes Annual Report on Exchange Arrangements (AREAER) — use to classify current regimes.
- **Carry trade sizing:** Reduce FX carry position size when counterparty is a managed floater (partial trilemma binding) — carry is less stable.
- **Event risk:** Peg breaks are the biggest tail risk in FX carry. Monitor reserve adequacy and current account deficits in pegged currencies.
