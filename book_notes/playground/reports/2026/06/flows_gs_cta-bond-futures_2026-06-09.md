## 1. Executive summary
Goldman Sachs' FICC & Equities Futures Sales desk in Japan circulates this weekly note on 9 June 2026 (data as of 8 June) to give Japan-domestic institutional clients color on where systematic trend-following funds (CTAs) are estimated to be positioned in global government bond futures, and how that positioning is likely to evolve under different price scenarios over the next week and month. The note relays a US strategy team's proprietary simulated model, which infers implied net CTA length (in \$m/DV01) per market from historical price action against undisclosed momentum thresholds, and projects forward flow under base/up/down price scenarios at 1-week and 1-month horizons. On this reading, the desk argues CTA books remain net short global bonds and near the bottom of their trailing 1-year range, but this week's US payrolls report reversed Europe's earlier threshold breakout without pulling the US or Japan legs back toward theirs — so the forward-flow scenarios that a week ago pointed toward broadening short-covering have compressed sharply, and the market's prior asymmetric setup is shifting toward a flatter, more range-bound profile.

## 2. Methodology & reasoning (PEE blocks + bulleted causal chains)

- **Point:** The CTA book is still net short and still deep in its own historical range — the "big picture" hasn't changed even though the marginal flow has turned slightly positive.
  **Evidence:** Current net position −\$128.9m DV01, versus −\$138.8m the prior week (a ~\$9.9m reduction in shortness); the note states this level remains in the lower part of the trailing 1-year simulated net-length range (Figure 4).
  **Chain:** A multi-month CTA short-building phase appears to have exhausted itself ("sold out and stationary," per the note) → a small covering correction begins this week → but the absolute level stays near 1-year lows → **directionally the book is still short-dominant even as the pace of shorting decelerates.**
  **Read:** Purely descriptive — a model-implied position level, not a statistical estimate with a confidence interval. This is the "stock" that every later flow number in the note is a marginal change against.

- **Point:** A regional divergence — "Europe leads, US/JGB lags" — is the report's actual explanatory mechanism, and it is the reason the covering trade stalled this week rather than broadened.
  **Evidence:** Europe (Bund/RX, Bobl/OE, Schatz/DU) plus Gilt had cleared their short-term momentum thresholds as of the prior week; despite a global rates selloff on the NFP print, they held relatively stable near/at that threshold rather than reversing outright. The US (2y/5y/10y/30y futures: TU/FV/TY/US) and JGB, by contrast, remained short-biased and below their thresholds throughout. The **1-week base-case per-contract split** makes the divergence concrete: RX +0.9m, OE +1.0m, Gilt +1.0m — i.e. Germany and the Gilt supply essentially all of the +\$4.5m — against the desk's explicit note that in the US 「US +1.3m以外動意⾒込まれず」 ("no movement expected other than US +1.3m") and JGB −0.1m. Three of the four US contracts and the JGB are forecast to do *nothing* over the coming week.
  **Chain:** NFP triggers a global rates selloff → Europe's fragile, already-thin threshold breakout absorbs the shock without fully reversing → the US and JGB, further from their own thresholds, get pushed back further into short territory → **the region that was ahead (Europe) stalls while the laggards (US/JGB) fall further behind, so the covering trade does not broaden globally this week.**
  **Read:** This is the load-bearing claim of the entire note — every downstream scenario number is a direct consequence of this one regional split — but it rests entirely on an undisclosed threshold-construction methodology (referenced only via the "Momentum Thresholds by Market" chart caption, Figure 7); there is no stated lookback window, moving-average convention, or volatility-scaling rule to independently verify it.

- **Point:** Forward-flow projections compressed at both horizons this week, and the market's prior directional asymmetry (limited downside, accelerating upside) is normalizing into a flatter, two-sided profile.
  **Evidence:** 1-week base case +\$11.9m → +\$4.5m (still buying, decelerating); 1-week Up +\$30.9m → +\$20.8m (still room, smaller — led by RX +5.1m, OE +2.2m, Gilt +3.2m, again Europe); 1-week Down +\$3.3m → −\$1.1m (flips slightly negative — the market no longer "buys even on a selloff"). 1-month base case +\$36.7m → −\$3.8m (flips to net sell, with the US mixed — TU −1.0m, FV +0.0m, TY +3.0m, US +2.7m — and Europe relapsing below threshold: RX −2.2m, OE −2.2m, DU −1.9m, Gilt −2.1m, against JGB +3.5m); 1-month Up +\$185.4m → +\$135.3m (broad covering potential retained, smaller — RX +28.8m, OE +20.0m, DU +8.7m, US +29.6m, TY +11.5m, JGB +12.2m); 1-month Down +\$7.6m → −\$4.5m (flips to sell-dominant).
  **Chain:** The stalled threshold recapture from the pillar above → all three scenario branches (base/up/down) shrink in magnitude at both the 1-week and 1-month horizon → the Down scenario flips from consistently positive at both horizons (a one-sided "CTAs buy no matter what" structure) to mildly negative → **the prior asymmetric setup is normalizing into a range where CTA flow no longer reliably cushions a selloff or reliably accelerates a rally.**
  **Read:** Unusually quantified for a sales note — three explicit numerical scenarios per horizon, directly comparable week-over-week — but all six numbers (three scenarios × two horizons) descend from the same single undisclosed model, so they corroborate each other by construction rather than serving as independent confirmations.

- **Point:** *(added on the 2026-08-11 re-read — not in the first pass.)* The base case and the Up scenario disagree about which region matters, and that disagreement is the most transferable thing in the note: **distance below a trigger is latent covering capacity**, so the market that does least in the base case does most in the tail.
  **Evidence:** In the 1-month base case the US complex is inert-to-mixed (TU −1.0m, FV +0.0m, TY +3.0m, US +2.7m; net +\$4.7m) and Europe is the drag (RX/OE/DU/Gilt all negative, net −\$8.4m). Flip to the Up scenario and the ordering inverts: the single largest contributor is **US +29.6m** — larger than any European contract, RX +28.8m included — with TY +11.5m behind it, so the US complex supplies ≈\$41m of the +\$135.3m against Europe's ≈\$57.5m from three contracts. The leg the desk says will do nothing next week is the leg with the most to do if the market rallies.
  **Chain:** US/JGB sit furthest *below* their thresholds → in the base and down paths they simply stay short, contributing ~0 flow → but a rally must traverse that entire distance to reach the trigger, and the accumulated short is largest exactly where the distance is greatest → **conditional flow is convex in distance-to-threshold: the laggard contributes least in the modal path and most in the tail.**
  **Read:** Inferential in a way the note never states — the desk reports the scenario grid but does not draw this out, and it is the reason a "nothing to see here" base case is not the same as "no risk here." This is the one genuinely reusable mechanism in the note, and it strengthens rather than merely illustrates `IDEA-036`: threshold proximity governs *synchronization*, threshold **distance** governs *conditional magnitude*. They are two different statistics off the same measurement.

- **Point:** *(opened on the 2026-08-11 re-read; **resolved on the 2026-08-15 re-read**.)* No scenario headline equals the sum of its published parts — and extending the check from one cell to all four shows the gap is **systematically an omitted set of markets, not an arithmetic error**. The note's conclusion is therefore reproducible in *direction* but not in *attribution*: at the 1-month horizon, the markets the note names sum to flat, and the entire headline is carried by markets it never names.
  **Evidence:** Every scenario cell for which the desk publishes a breakdown, reconciled against its own headline (\$m DV01; the note names nine contracts in total — TU/FV/TY/US, RX/OE/DU, Gilt, JGB):

  | Cell | Headline | Σ published components | Residual | as % of headline | Contracts given |
  |---|--:|--:|--:|--:|:--:|
  | 1-week base | **+4.5** | +4.1 | **+0.4** | 9% | 5 of 9 (desk states TU/FV/TY ≈ 0, so only DU is silent) |
  | 1-week Up | **+20.8** | +10.5 | **+10.3** | 50% | 3 of 9 (desk writes 「…中⼼」 — "centred on", explicitly partial) |
  | 1-month base | **−3.8** | **−0.2** | **−3.6** | **95%** | **9 of 9 — the complete named set** |
  | 1-month Up | **+135.3** | +110.8 | **+24.5** | 18% | 6 of 9 (「…に加え」 — "in addition to", explicitly partial) |

  **Chain:** A residual appears in all four cells → in every one of the four its **sign matches the headline's sign** (+/+, +/+, −/−, +/+) → an arithmetic or transcription inconsistency would produce sign-random, small residuals, whereas a set of *additional markets responding to the same price path* produces exactly this sign-coherent, scenario-scaling pattern → the residual **behaves like a market, not like an error** → **the model spans markets the prose never comments on** (Figure 1 is captioned "by Country/Region" while the commentary covers nine contracts — Australian and Canadian futures are the obvious candidates). The 1-month base case is the diagnostic cell: it is the only one where all nine named contracts are given, they sum to −\$0.2m (flat), and the −\$3.8m headline is therefore ≈95% supplied by the uncommented remainder.
  **Read:** This *resolves* the 2026-08-11 finding in the note's favour on integrity and against it on usability. The desk is not making an arithmetic mistake; it is reporting a model whose universe is wider than its prose. But the consequence for a reader is sharper than "opaque model": **the note's single load-bearing number — the +\$36.7m → −\$3.8m sign flip that its whole conclusion rests on — is produced almost entirely by markets the reader is never shown.** On the evidence actually published, the nine named contracts say *flat*, not *small net selling*. Treat the 1-month sign flip as the model's claim, not the note's demonstration; the 1-week deceleration (+\$11.9m → +\$4.5m, 91% attributed) remains the better-supported half. The generalisable move is in the next section.

**Method extracted (transferable, and the main new yield of this pass): test the residual, not just the sum.** When a published breakdown fails to sum to its headline, the gap itself is evidence about *which* failure occurred, and the test is free:

- residual **sign-coherent across independent cells**, scaling with the scenario → an omitted component set. The claim is internally consistent; what you have lost is *attribution*, and the fraction of the headline you cannot attribute is the honest discount on it.
- residual **sign-random and small** → transcription or rounding. Ignore.
- residual **large and sign-incoherent** → the aggregate and the breakdown come from different runs or different vintages. Discard the headline.

Two cells are enough to run it, and it converts "the numbers don't add up" from a complaint into a measurement. Recorded as a third source on `IDEA-028`.

**Weakest link:** The entire note rests on one proprietary, undisclosed "momentum threshold" model. The actual signal construction (lookback windows, moving-average or channel-breakout convention, volatility scaling) is never stated, even in outline, and the note's own disclaimer says the simulated backtest is "for illustrative purposes only" with "no assurance... that the strategy will operate or would have operated... in a manner consistent with the above analysis" — there is no way to independently check whether week-over-week changes reflect real fund flow or the model's own recalibration. *(2026-08-15: the opacity now has a measured size. The model's universe is wider than its prose, and the unattributed remainder runs 9–50% of each scenario headline and **95% of the 1-month base case that carries the conclusion**. So the weakest link is not just "you cannot see the construction" but "you cannot see which markets are speaking" — and on the markets you *can* see, the 1-month claim does not hold.)*

No formula is reproduced here: this note has no disclosed accounting identity or estimator to write out (unlike the debt-dynamics or wage-regression math in the Japan Outlook digest) — its "model" is a black box by design, which is itself the methodological finding worth recording.

## Retrospective — how the call aged (added 2026-08-11, ~9 weeks after publication)

**What can and cannot be scored.** The note's *flow* forecasts are unscoreable: nobody outside GS has the CTA position series, and the model is undisclosed, so the −\$128.9m level and the DV01 scenario grid cannot be marked. What *is* scoreable is the note's **conditional precondition**, which it states explicitly: 「⽶国（TU/FV/TY/US）およびJGBが閾値を明確に奪回しない限り、グローバルでのカバー加速には⾄らず」 — *absent a clear US/JGB threshold recapture, global covering does not accelerate.* Yield direction is a legitimate proxy for that precondition, since a futures contract cannot cross an upside momentum trigger while its yield is rising.

| | At note (2026-06-09) | Now (2026-07-31) | Move |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| UST 2y | 4.15% | 4.23% | **+8bp** |
| UST 10y | 4.56% | 4.68% | **+12bp** |
| UST 30y | 5.03% | 5.21% | **+18bp** |
| JGB 10y | 2.65% (May obs) | 2.67% (Jun obs) | **+2bp** |

**Verdict: the precondition held, so the conditional was right — and it was the cautious half of the note that paid.** Yields rose across the US curve over the following nine weeks, with the long end leading (+18bp). US futures therefore moved *further* below their triggers, not toward them; JGB likewise drifted higher. On the note's own logic the Up scenario (+\$135.3m of broad covering) never armed, and the "range-bound, neither side accelerating" base case was the correct read. Note what this does and does not vindicate: **the desk's conditional structure was sound; the model's point forecasts remain untested.** The valuable output was the *if–then*, not the DV01s — which is the same lesson the fourth pillar draws from the Up-scenario asymmetry.

**One caution against over-crediting this.** A 9-week window with one directional outcome is a single observation, and "yields rose so the laggards stayed laggards" is close to tautological given how the threshold is defined. This is a consistency check, not evidence of forecasting skill, and it should not raise any `IDEAS.md` score. Recorded because the library's stage-3 roadmap wants dated claims scored against outcomes, not because one hit means anything.
