# Report Digest: Goldman Sachs — GOAL Kickstart: "Tracking Micro and Macro Risks into the Summer Holidays" (2026)

- **Source:** `book_notes/playground/reports/2026/07/crossasset_gs_goal-kickstart_2026-07-27.pdf`
- **Type / issuer:** Sell-side cross-asset strategy (GOAL Kickstart, weekly) — Goldman Sachs Portfolio Strategy Research (Mueller-Glissmann, Giglio, Ferrario, Ferrannini, Porfidia, Oppenheimer)
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30 (report published 27 July 2026, 4:35pm BST)
- **One-line claim:** GS stays tactically Neutral over 3m / modestly pro-risk over 12m despite an oil-driven inflation-and-rates scare and record-high investor equity allocations, preferring convexity (long-dated equity calls, short-dated S&P puts, long JPY) and real-asset/style diversification over adding outright equity beta or credit risk.
- **Decision it changes:** Whether to keep running unhedged, beta-heavy risk exposure through Q3 versus adding convexity and diversification — the report argues current positioning (crowded equity longs, tight credit spreads, elevated risk-appetite z-scores) plus a fresh macro right-tail (oil, rates) argues for hedging the tails rather than either de-risking outright or riding long beta unhedged.
- **Scores (1–5):** Credibility 5 · Relevance 5 · Actionability 4 — GS's flagship weekly cross-asset dashboard, built on long-run, previously-documented systematic frameworks (risk-appetite indicator, PCA risk factors, logit-based recession/drawdown models) rather than one-off narrative; actionability is docked one point because the note references but doesn't restate the full construction methodology for its core indicators (GSRAI, PCA), deferring to earlier GOAL editions not included here.

## 1. Executive summary
GS's Portfolio Strategy team publishes this weekly "Kickstart" note on 27 July 2026 to frame tactical asset allocation heading into the least liquid, most event-dense stretch of the summer — FOMC/BoE/BoJ decisions, the busiest week of Q2 earnings, and a sharp two-sided oil shock (Brent/WTI up ~30% cumulatively over three weeks before reversing on a US–Iran ceasefire). The methodology is a standing cross-asset dashboard rather than a single thesis test — a proprietary Risk Appetite Indicator and its PCA decomposition (global growth, monetary policy, dollar, euro-area-risk factors), multivariate logit models for recession and S&P 500 drawdown/rally probabilities, valuation-percentile tables across equities/credit/bonds, and positioning/flow/correlation monitors, all read together to set the house asset-allocation grid (Exhibit 8). GS argues markets have shifted focus from idiosyncratic AI/tech-earnings risk toward macro right-tail risk — oil-driven inflation and a more hawkish repricing of central-bank paths than its own economists expect — and on that basis holds equities tactically Neutral for 3 months / Overweight for 12 months, stays Underweight credit on thin default compensation, and expresses its risk-management stance through option convexity rather than a change in headline weights.

## 2. Methodology & reasoning (PEE blocks + bulleted causal chains)

- **Point:** Positioning is stretched, but on a genuine growth signal rather than blind froth — which is why GS hedges tails instead of de-risking outright.
  **Evidence:** GS Risk Appetite Indicator (GSRAI, construction detailed in the July 2016 GOAL edition, not restated here) sits at ≈+1 on a 1-year z-score, the upper edge of its historical band. Its PCA decomposition (April 2019 GOAL edition) shows PC1 (global growth) elevated and tracking a "Global MAP Score" leading-growth proxy, while PC2 (monetary policy) and PC3 (dollar) sit closer to neutral.
  **Chain:** Global growth proxy (PC1) runs hot → risk appetite (GSRAI) is pulled to the top of its multi-year range → but the policy (PC2) and dollar (PC3) components stay neutral, i.e. this isn't a liquidity-driven melt-up → **euphoric positioning is real but growth-grounded, so the prescription is "hedge the tails," not "sell the risk."**
  **Read:** Descriptive z-score reads on proprietary, non-public composites — not independently replicable. Both GSRAI and the PCA decomposition are cited to two much earlier GOAL editions (2016, 2019) rather than reconstructed here, so their current validity has to be taken on faith.

- **Point:** The ~20–25% probability of a >20% 12-month S&P drawdown is a valuation story, not a growth or sentiment story — a specific, falsifiable claim, not a vague "risk is elevated."
  **Evidence:** A multivariate logit model for S&P 500 drawdown/rally probability, decomposed via Shapley values into four driver buckets (valuations, leading growth, business cycle, sentiment). S&P 500 sits at the 58th percentile of 10-year forward P/E history; IG/HY credit spreads (US/EUR) sit at the 91st–98th percentile — near their most expensive decile.
  **Chain:** Shapley decomposition attributes the ~20–25% drawdown probability almost entirely to the "valuations" bucket, not growth or sentiment deterioration → equities are only moderately rich (58th %ile) while credit is priced for near-zero risk (91st–98th %ile) → that wedge is the direct justification for staying **Underweight credit** (thin compensation given year-end HY default forecasts raised to 4% US / 5% Europe) while staying **constructive on equities via convexity** rather than raw beta.
  **Read:** This is the most inferential piece of the note (a fitted logit model with an explicit feature-attribution step), but Shapley attributions over correlated macro-financial features are sensitive to how the decomposition handles that correlation — the note presents "driven by valuations" as a clean, singular read without discussing that sensitivity.

- **Point:** The proximate trigger for the whole note — an oil shock plus a Fed-repricing gap — is a near-term, falsifiable catalyst, not a slow-moving macro drift.
  **Evidence:** Brent/WTI up ~30% cumulatively over three weeks, reversing on a US–Iran ceasefire. GS's rates team: market pricing implies what they call the largest non-cut "surprise" in decades if the Fed holds; GS's own economists forecast the Fed on hold through year-end (11% probability in their point forecast) against a 15% Bloomberg-consensus median and a ~20% GS-model-implied recession probability — i.e., the GS *model* reads more cautious than the GS *economists*.
  **Chain:** Oil spikes → inflation-scare repricing pushes market-implied Fed policy more hawkish than GS's own economists expect → this repricing gap resolves one way or the other at the July FOMC meeting (this week, per the note) → **a genuinely falsifiable, days-away catalyst**, which is why the note frames this as a live risk to monitor rather than a settled call.
  **Read:** Descriptive market-pricing comparison, not a model output — but usefully specific: "GS thinks the market is wrong about the Fed" is dated and falsifiable within days, unlike most macro calls in this genre.

- **Point:** The note's own numbers contain an unreconciled tension between its structural FX view and its tactical hedge — worth flagging as a critique, not just a summary point.
  **Evidence:** GS's 12-month FX forecast has USD/JPY drifting higher to ¥165 (a weaker yen, consistent with continued rate-differential widening, Exhibit 94) — yet the note's recommended tail hedge is long JPY calls (a bet on yen *strength*).
  **Chain:** Structural 12m view: rate differentials widen → yen weakens to ¥165 → but the tactical hedge is sized for the opposite move (a shock-scenario yen spike) → the note never explicitly frames this as a base-case-vs-tail-hedge distinction → **a reader following the structural view and the hedge recommendation literally would be positioned against their own house forecast**, unless the hedge is understood purely as tail insurance rather than a directional view.
  **Read:** This is a genuine internal inconsistency (or at minimum an unexplained framing gap) rather than a flaw in either individual claim — exactly the kind of leap a critical reader should catch rather than pass through as "GS both loves and hedges the yen."

**Weakest link:** The Shapley "valuations bucket" attribution (pillar 2) — Shapley decompositions of correlated macro-financial features are attribution-order-sensitive, and the note presents the split as clean and singular without discussing that sensitivity; this is the most load-bearing inferential claim in the note (it justifies the entire UW-credit / convexity-over-beta stance) and the least interrogated.

**Take.** (1) [IDEA-017](../../IDEAS.md#idea-017): when two claims on the same issuer cash flows sit at very different percentiles of their own histories, the SPREAD is the signal, not either level — take corporate risk in the instrument where you are actually paid for it, because a mandate-driven, ratings-bucketed reach-for-yield bid compresses the capped claim and cannot touch the uncapped one. Generalises to preferreds vs common, sub debt vs equity, mezz vs equity tranche. Buildable now: BAMLH0A0HYM2 and BAMLC0A0CM both resolve through get_data (the source digest wrongly listed the credit leg as a gap); only the 12m forward-P/E leg is missing — substitute Shiller CAPE or earnings yield minus real yield. Carry the pool's caveat too: forward-P/E and OAS percentiles are not on a common risk-premium scale, so the clean version compares equity risk premium to spread-implied default compensation net of expected loss. (2) [IDEA-027](../../IDEAS.md#idea-027): decompose a risk estimate before choosing the hedge instrument — the level tells you how worried to be, the attribution tells you whether to buy convexity or cut beta. Applies to any drawdown or regime output his own review pipeline emits. (3) Keep Exhibits 21–22 specifically because they cut against the pool's own #1 idea: vol-target and momentum overlays on 60/40 underperformed static 60/40 in exactly the positive-correlation regime where [IDEA-001](../../IDEAS.md#idea-001) prescribes buying trend diversification. Counter-evidence from the same note that supplies [IDEA-001](../../IDEAS.md#idea-001)'s empirical anchor is worth more than either alone. (4) The reading template: a ¥165 weaker-yen 12m forecast printed alongside a long-JPY-call tail hedge, never framed as base-case-vs-tail-insurance — the standing audit for any note where the forecast table and the trade recommendation come from different desks.

**Ignore.** The entire GSRAI/PCA layer and everything downstream — the +1 z-score read, the PC1-elevated / PC2-PC3-neutral decomposition, and [IDEA-044](../../IDEAS.md#idea-044) itself; the pool's verdict is that a public proxy 'is not the thing being read, so any result is a statement about the proxy.'
