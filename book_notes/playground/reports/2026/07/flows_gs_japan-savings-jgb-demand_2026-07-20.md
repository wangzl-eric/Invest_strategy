## 1. Executive summary

Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research published this *Global Markets Daily* on 20 July 2026, days after Finance Minister Katayama and Prime Minister Takaichi signalled that Tokyo is considering ways to redirect domestic savings into domestic assets, to answer a specific client question: whether two named measures — tilting GPIF's \$2.8tn portfolio toward its 31% domestic-bond ceiling, and admitting JGBs directly into the NISA tax wrapper — can structurally support the JGB market. The method is a flow-sizing exercise rather than a forecast: GS decomposes GPIF's holdings from its annual ISIN-level disclosures (JGBs 80–90% of domestic bonds, USTs ~50% of foreign bonds), separates *mechanical* rebalancing flow from *discretionary* room under the ±6ppt band, strips valuation effects out of curve-bucket net purchases to establish a long-end tilt, then maps the resulting quantity into price through an unspecified in-house swap-spread framework calibrated on UST/Bund/Gilt free-float sensitivity, and sizes NISA off the JSDA traded-value waterfall. GS argues the GPIF lever is worth only **3–6bp on 30y swap spreads** — against an **8bp round trip in that same spread in the week of writing** — and is delivered over **2–4 years** at the observed 1–2ppt/year adjustment speed, while NISA is dismissed on a behavioural reading (low household JGB allocations reflect historically low *yields*, not tax status, and per the Italy analogue rising retail ownership accompanies *rising* yields). The note therefore concludes that both measures work through **signalling and crowding-in rather than outright purchases**, with the tactical residual that **20s and 30s have more room to richen alongside wider 20y/30y swap spreads** while macro-cheapened 10s and the belly do not. Confidence is asserted throughout and never quantified — there is not one confidence interval, standard error, sample period, or estimation window in the document, and the framework carrying the entire conclusion is invoked twice without a citation.

## 2. Methodology & reasoning

Four pillars carry the note. Pillar 1 is the GPIF quantity, pillar 2 is the quantity→price transmission (and the one that does not reconcile), pillar 3 is NISA, pillar 4 is the curve recommendation — which is also where GS declines to own its own conclusion.

### Pillar 1 — The discretionary lever is smaller than the mechanical flow it would add to

- **Point:** GPIF's *passive* rebalancing already buys more JGBs per year than the entire discretionary room allows, so a "pro-active reallocation" changes **signalling, not quantity**.
- **Evidence:** AUM ≈ **\$2.8tn** (end-March 2026), benchmark **25%** in each of domestic bonds / foreign bonds / domestic equity / foreign equity, domestic-bond band **±6ppt** (19–31%, narrowed over time, Exhibit 2), strategy next reviewed **2030**. Current domestic-bond share **27%**, ceiling **31%**, stated room ≈ **\$75bn** (= JPY12.0tn at 160). JGBs are **80–90%** of domestic bonds; USTs ≈ **50%** of foreign bonds, USTs+EGBs > 80% since 2019 (Exhibit 3). GPIF owns ≈ **5% of the JGB market**, **just under 1%** of UST/Bund/Gilt. Exhibit 5 (2016–2026, JPYtn): the 2026 domestic-bond rebalancing bar reaches ≈ **+17 JPYtn** against a red-dashed *"JGB purchase capacity"* line at ≈ **+11.5 JPYtn** (2024 bar ≈ +15, 2025 ≈ +7.5). Exhibit 6 scatter (JGB rebalancing flow, JPYtn, vs FX & bond returns, %) has a clearly **negative** slope running from about (−15%, +13.5tn) to (+15%, −8tn).
- **Chain:** Foreign assets and domestic equities outperform while JGBs underperform on rising yields and a weaker JPY → the domestic-bond share drifts **down** mechanically → GPIF must buy JGBs just to hold the share flat → that passive buying (≈ JPY17tn in 2026) **already exceeds the entire discretionary room** (≈ JPY11.5–12tn) → a pro-active reallocation adds no marginal quantity the market was not already receiving → **whatever the announcement is worth, it is worth it as a signal about government intent, not as a flow.**
- **Read:** Descriptive, load-bearing, and the **strongest pillar in the note** — it is the only one built on auditable regulatory data (GPIF publishes ISIN-level holdings annually since 2019, footnote 1). Two leaps, both unflagged and both cutting GS's way if it had taken them:
  - GS states Exhibit 6's negative-feedback structure and then **does not follow it**. If flow is a *decreasing* function of returns, the flow is **stabilising** and can never be the marginal driver of a trend. That is a stronger argument for the note's own conclusion than anything it actually writes, and it is left on the table.
  - The same negative slope means a yen **rally** mechanically pushes the domestic-bond share **up** toward the 31% ceiling and turns GPIF into a JGB **seller**. The note assumes the weak-yen state persists and never prices its own mechanism running in reverse. **It has already run in reverse: USD/JPY 162.51 on publication → 157.40 on 2-Aug, −3.15%**, which on my arithmetic has consumed ~10% of the 4ppt room in ten sessions (see *Arithmetic*, item 7).
  - Scale of the mechanical channel, from the slope I fitted off the chart (**b ≈ −0.65 to −0.72 JPYtn per 1%**): a −10% year in FX-and-bond returns generates **+6.5–7.2 JPYtn ≈ \$41–45bn** of JGB buying automatically — **54–60% of the entire \$75bn "policy lever", with no policy.**

### Pillar 2 — The 3–6bp does not reconcile with the note's own inputs (understated quantity × overstated sensitivity)

- **Point:** The number that carries every "this is small" framing in the note is built on a quantity 17–33% below what its own AUM and band imply (17–26% below the JGB-share-adjusted \$90–101bn, 33% below the \$112bn gross), and a sensitivity 3–5× above its own stated baseline.
- **Evidence:** Stated transmission, verbatim: *"For USTs, Bunds and Gilts, our swap spread frameworks estimate that a 1ppt change in 'free float' (the share of debt owned by the private sector) is worth around 1bp on 30y swap spreads, but the impact could be larger (e.g. 2-4bp) if flows are disproportionately directed into 30y bonds or catalyses more 'crowding in' from other domestic buyers beyond any direct GPIF flows."* — note that the crowding-in case is explicitly **third-party** demand, not GPIF's own quantity. Applied: *"Shifting from the current domestic bond share of 27% to 31% … and around USD75bn in addition JGB purchases) would represent around 3-6bp in 30y spreads—this compares with an 8bp round trip in 30y swap spreads this week (Exhibit 9)."* Foreign ownership of JGBs ≈ **14%**, among the lowest in G10. Exhibit 9 (30y JGB swap spreads, Jan-25→Jul-26, axis −20 to −100bp): start ≈ **−38bp**, trough ≈ **−92bp** (Apr-25), ≈ **−60/−65bp** as recently as Jun-26, ending ≈ **−30bp** — **the least-negative (richest) print in the entire 18-month window**. Exhibit 9's caption says the move was **8–10bp**; the body says 8bp.
- **Chain:** 4ppt of \$2.8tn is **\$112bn**, and **\$90–101bn** after the note's own 80–90% JGB haircut → but the note prints **\$75bn**, which requires JGBs to be only **67%** of the incremental domestic-bond allocation, below its own stated range → separately, \$75bn against the free float implied by the note's own "5% of the JGB market" statistic (GPIF JGB ≈ \$643bn → market ≈ \$12.9tn; ex a ~50% BoJ holding, free float ≈ \$6.4tn) is **Δφ ≈ 1.17ppt** → at the stated baseline β = 1bp/ppt that is **1.2bp, not 3–6bp** → printing 3–6bp requires **β_eff = 2.6–5.1 bp/ppt**, i.e. the top of and beyond the crowding-in range → **the headline is a crowding-in scenario presented as a base case, built on an understated quantity.**
- **Read:** Inferential, and **the single most load-bearing claim in the note** — every "this is small" framing is scaled by it — yet the least specified in it. The "swap spread frameworks" are invoked twice with **no sample period, no estimation window, no functional form, no standard error, no citation**, then **transplanted** from UST/Bund/Gilt onto JGBs while the note itself concedes *"the sensitivities of JGB swap spreads to debt supply have historically been a little lower than in other markets."* That is a downward adjustment acknowledged in prose and then **not applied to the arithmetic**. The two errors happen to **offset** — understated $Q$, overstated $\beta$ — which is exactly why the number looks plausible. Run both on the note's own inputs and the honest baseline is **≈1.75bp**, roughly one-fifth of a single week's realised move.

### Pillar 3 — NISA is a rounding error, and the behavioural argument against it is the note's best insight

- **Point:** Making JGBs NISA-eligible cannot move the market, because the constraint on household JGB holdings is the **yield**, not the **tax**.
- **Evidence:** Over **20mn** NISA accounts, over **JPY71tn** in traded value as of end-2025. Exhibit 10 waterfall (Jan–May 2026): growth quota **75%** : Tsumitate quota 25%; within growth quota, equities **59%** : investment trusts 41%; within growth-quota equities, **domestic 93%** : foreign 7%; within total NISA traded value, domestic equities **41%** : investment trusts **56%**. JGB **funds** are already eligible in the growth quota; **direct** JGBs are not (the Tsumitate quota is restricted to low-cost diversified equity mutual funds). Direct household JGB ownership ≈ **4% of portfolio assets** after stripping out NISA. Italy analogue, verbatim: *"increasing retail ownership is typically associated with rising, not falling, yields."*
- **Chain:** Growth quota = 0.75 × JPY71tn = **JPY53.25tn** → households currently put **93%** of their growth-quota equity money into **domestic equities** while JGB funds sit **already eligible and unbought** → the revealed preference is not tax-constrained, it is yield-constrained → apply the observed 4% direct-JGB portfolio weight to the growth quota and incremental demand is **JPY2.13tn ≈ \$13bn**, **18%** of the GPIF lever and **≈0.21ppt** of free float → **0.21–0.83bp** on 30y swap spreads under the note's own β range → **NISA eligibility is not a market event, and causality runs yields → retail ownership, not the reverse.**
- **Read:** Corroborating rather than load-bearing (pillar 1 carries the note), but **the most intellectually honest passage in it** — it identifies the direction of causality and refuses the flattering interpretation, and it extends the same logic to GPIF (*"substantial rebalancing flows are a consequence of rising yields and JPY underperformance, rather than a driver of JGB outperformance"*). Two leaps:
  - The Italy result is cited as *"as we have shown"* with **no coefficient, no sample, no period** — an external result asserted as settled.
  - **"4% of portfolio assets" is never defined.** 4% of gross household financial assets and 4% of non-cash investable assets differ by roughly an order of magnitude in Japan, and the entire NISA sizing depends on which one it means.
  - Credibility mark that is real: **Exhibit 10 fully reconciles.** I re-derived every cell from the conditional shares and all tie to ≤0.2pp (item 9 below). The one table in the note that can be independently audited is clean.

### Pillar 4 — The curve call rests on a fact deployed in two opposite directions, and GS will not put it in its own book

- **Point:** "20s/30s richen, 10s stay cheap" is asserted directionally on evidence the note itself calls ambiguous two pages later.
- **Evidence:** Pages 3–4, Exhibits 7–8 show GPIF, valuation-stripped, *"selling its shorter maturity bonds (sub-2y) and purchasing longer maturities"*, used to argue *"at least some capacity to direct flows towards long-end bonds, which is where we have argued there is probably the most 'bang for the buck' for the JPY curve."* Page 5, on the same behaviour: *"Given the revealed behaviour to rebalancing by selling short maturity bonds, it is also not clear this would have a major curve impact."* Exhibit 4 (WAM of GPIF sovereign holdings, described in its own title as *"relatively steady"*): JGBs ≈ **10.7yr (2020) → ≈9.7yr (2026)**; Gilts **21yr → ≈12.3yr**; USTs flat ≈7.5–8yr; EGBs ≈10.5 → ≈9.3. Exhibit 7 (JGBs, JPYtn) shows the sub-2y net-flow bucket **near zero**; Exhibit 8 (USTs, USDbn) shows it clearly negative at **≈ −15 to −22/yr**. Conclusion offered, p.1 bullet: *"We see more room for 20s and 30s to outperform alongside wider 20y and 30y swap spreads"*; p.6 body: *"Given more supply-related cheapening at the very long end, we think 20s and 30s have more room to richen, alongside 20y and 30y swap spreads. The macro-led cheapening in 10s and the belly are less likely to be supported, as evidenced by persistent 10s30s flattening."* — quoted in full because the flattening evidence attaches to the **10s-stay-cheap** leg, not to the 20s/30s richening leg it is often read as supporting. **Best Trade Ideas: 13 trades, zero in JGBs, JPY rates, or anything Japan.**
- **Chain:** GPIF's WAM is roughly constant → holding WAM constant against roll-down requires continuously buying longer maturities → GPIF has a long-end tilt → incremental JGB buying lands at 20y/30y where "bang for the buck" is highest → **therefore 20s/30s richen and 20y/30y swap spreads widen** → *but* the same sub-2y-selling behaviour is then used to conclude the curve impact is unclear → **the conclusion as written is directional; the conclusion as evidenced is ambiguous, and the ambiguity is never resolved.**
- **Read:** Inferential, corroborating, and the **weakest-evidenced pillar**. Three separate problems:
  - **The "steady WAM" claim is false as stated for JGBs.** 10.7yr → 9.7yr is a ~1-year decline over six years of roll-down, meaning GPIF replaces only **~83%** of the annual maturity decay. The long-end tilt is real but **incomplete and decaying** — the supporting fact is weaker than the headline it supports.
  - **The sub-2y-selling evidence is strong in USTs and weak-to-absent in JGBs** (Exhibit 7's sub-2y bucket is near zero), yet the text says it shows *"this for both."*
  - **The loudest item is the omission.** GIR wrote five pages concluding 20s/30s should richen and did not add it to a 13-trade book that includes NZD and CAD 2s10s steepeners, INR 30y bonds, a Nifty Banks/Pharma pair, and short SGD/MYR. A house that will run a 2s10s CAD steepener at a 3.9:1 printed reward:risk but not its own five-page Japan conclusion is telling you the conviction is a page-6 sentence, not a position. Contrast the same firm's FICC desks in April, which carried **seven** simultaneous expressions of a short-10y-JGB view.

### Load-bearing formulas

**The transmission model (stated only in prose — this is the whole note in two lines):**

$$\Delta \mathrm{SS}_{30y} \;=\; \beta \cdot \Delta\varphi, \qquad \Delta\varphi \;=\; \frac{Q}{F}\times 100 \ \text{(ppt)}$$

where $\mathrm{SS}_{30y}$ is the 30y swap spread (bp; JGB yield minus swap rate, negative = bond cheap to swaps), $\varphi$ the **free float** (share of outstanding government debt held by the private sector, %), $\beta$ the estimated sensitivity ($\beta \approx 1$ bp/ppt baseline, $2$–$4$ if concentrated in 30y or if crowding-in occurs), $Q$ the incremental official purchase (USD) and $F$ the free-float outstanding (USD).

**Mechanical room under the mandate (what \$75bn should be):**

$$Q_{\max} \;=\; \bigl(w_{\max} - w_{0}\bigr)\, A \,\theta$$

with $w_{\max}=0.31$, $w_{0}=0.27$, $A=\$2.8\mathrm{tn}$, $\theta\in[0.80,0.90]$ the JGB share of domestic bonds. $\Rightarrow Q_{\max} = 0.04 \times 2800 \times \theta = \$90\text{–}101\mathrm{bn}$ (\$112bn at $\theta=1$). **The note prints \$75bn, which requires $\theta = 0.67$.**

**Why rebalancing flow exists at all — the drift identity GS charts (Exhibits 5–6) and never writes down:**

$$w_i' \;=\; \frac{w_i\,(1+r_i)}{\sum_j w_j\,(1+r_j)}, \qquad \mathrm{Flow}_i \;=\; A'\,\bigl(w_i^{\text{target}} - w_i'\bigr)$$

with $w_i$ the start-of-period weight of sleeve $i \in \{\text{dom bond, for bond, dom eq, for eq}\}$, $r_i$ its JPY total return (FX-translated for foreign sleeves) and $A'$ end-of-period AUM. Domestic bonds have $r_{\text{DB}}<0$ when JGB yields rise, so $w_{\text{DB}}' < w_{\text{DB}}$ and $\mathrm{Flow}_{\text{DB}} > 0$: **GPIF mechanically buys what has fallen. That is a contrarian, stabilising flow — it cannot generate a trend.**

**Exhibit 6, fitted from the chart's own axes (GS plots the scatter and never states the slope):**

$$\mathrm{Flow}^{\mathrm{JGB}}_{t} \;\approx\; a \;+\; b\,R_t, \qquad \hat{b} \;\approx\; -0.65 \ \text{to} \ -0.72 \ \mathrm{JPYtn}\ \text{per } 1\%$$

read off the plotted trendline endpoints $(-15\%,\ +11 \text{ to } +13.5) \to (+15\%,\ -8.0)$, with $R_t$ the combined FX-and-bond return (%). This is a **chart read with roughly $\pm1.5$ JPYtn of resolution at each endpoint** — the $+14$ and $+12.5$ *data points* on the left of the cloud sit above the fitted line, not on it, so the magnitude is soft. **The sign is the finding, and it does not depend on the chart read at all: Exhibit 6's own title asserts flows are "generated by higher yields and a weaker currency", i.e. $b<0$.**

**NISA waterfall (Exhibit 10) as a product of conditional shares:**

$$V_{\text{dom eq}} \;=\; T \cdot s_{G} \cdot s_{E|G} \cdot s_{D|E,G}$$

with $T=\mathrm{JPY}71\mathrm{tn}$, $s_G=0.75$, $s_{E|G}=0.59$, $s_{D|E,G}=0.93 \Rightarrow V_{\text{dom eq}} = \mathrm{JPY}29.2\mathrm{tn} = 41.2\%$ of $T$. **Exhibit 10's independently-printed bottom row says 41% — it ties.**

**The signal-to-noise test the note sets up and does not run:**

$$\frac{\Delta \mathrm{SS}\,/\,\tau}{\sigma_{\text{weekly}}} \quad\text{with}\quad \tau = \frac{w_{\max}-w_0}{\dot{w}}$$

where $\Delta\mathrm{SS}=3$–$6$bp (total effect), $\dot{w}=1$–$2$ ppt/yr (observed adjustment speed) so $\tau=2$–$4$ years, and $\sigma_{\text{weekly}}=8$bp (the note's own one-week realised round trip). $\Rightarrow$ **0.014–0.058 bp/week of signal against 8bp/week of noise.**

### The arithmetic the note does not do

Every figure below is derived from the note's own stated inputs or from platform data pulled 2026-08-03.

1. **\$75bn does not tie — and it is the number every conclusion is scaled by.** 4ppt × \$2.8tn = **\$112bn**; the note's own 80–90% JGB share gives **\$90–101bn** (\$95bn at the midpoint). The printed **\$75bn** is **17–26% below that \$90–101bn band** (16.7% below \$90bn, 25.6% below \$101bn) and **33% below the \$112bn gross**, and it implies $\theta = 67\%$, below its own stated band. Inverting, \$75bn / 4ppt implies an AUM of **\$1.88tn**, not \$2.8tn. *Cross-check that does tie:* \$75bn at USD/JPY 160 = **JPY12.0tn**, matching Exhibit 5's red-dashed capacity line at ≈ +11.5 JPYtn — so \$75bn is internally consistent with the **chart** but not with the **text's** AUM × band arithmetic.
2. **Sensitivity of the verdict to that number.** Scaling 3–6bp linearly in $Q$: at \$95bn → **3.8–7.6bp**; at \$112bn → **4.5–9.0bp**. The note's entire "this is small" framing is the comparison to an **8bp** weekly round trip. **At the 4ppt-consistent quantity the effect straddles and exceeds its own comparator.** The headline verdict survives only because the quantity was understated.
3. **3–6bp also requires a β far above the stated baseline — the two errors offset.** GPIF JGB holdings = 0.27 × 0.85 × \$2.8tn = **\$643bn**; at "≈5% of the JGB market" that implies a JGB market of **\$12.9tn**; free float excludes the central bank, so at a 50% BoJ holding $F \approx$ **\$6.4tn**. Then $\Delta\varphi = 75/6{,}400 =$ **1.17ppt**, and at $\beta = 1$ the answer is **1.2bp**. To print 3–6bp you need **$\beta_{\text{eff}} = 2.6$–$5.1$ bp/ppt** (5.2–10.3 without stripping the BoJ). The note **understates $Q$ by ~33% and overstates $\beta$ by ~3–5×**, in offsetting directions it never reconciles. Run both honestly ($Q=\$112$bn, $\beta=1$): $\Delta\varphi = 1.75$ppt → **1.75bp**, about **one-fifth of a single week's realised move** (1.75/8 = 22%) and **~58% of the note's own 3bp floor** (29% of its 6bp ceiling).
4. **The pace arithmetic the note gestures at and never completes.** 4ppt at 1–2ppt/yr = **2–4 years**. Spread 3–6bp over that: **0.75–3.0 bp/year**, i.e. **0.014–0.058 bp/week** against the **8bp/week** the spread actually moved — **0.2%–0.7% signal-to-noise**. Even a full year at the fastest pace (3.0bp) is **37%** of one week's 8bp move (**19%** of the 16bp of total round-trip travel). Per week, the GPIF reallocation is, on the note's own numbers, **between 1/550th and 1/140th of the weekly noise** (0.014–0.058bp vs 8bp).
5. **Breakeven on trading the headline is negative.** Fundamental value of the full reallocation is 3–6bp delivered over 2–4 years; the headline itself moved the spread **8bp and round-tripped**. Entering on the announcement therefore has **negative expected value unless you fill within 3–6bp of the pre-headline level** — you pay up to 8bp of impact to capture 3–6bp of value on a 2–4 year deferral. **The announcement's noise amplitude exceeds its fair value.** Worse: Exhibit 9 puts the 30y spread at ≈ **−30bp**, the **richest level in the Jan-25→Jul-26 window** (trough ≈ −92bp; ≈ −60/−65bp as recently as Jun-26). GS recommends *wider* 20y/30y swap spreads **after ~30bp of richening — i.e. after 5–10× the entire fundamental value has already been priced.**
6. **The mechanical flow is 1.5× the discretionary lever.** Exhibit 5's 2026 domestic-bond bar (≈ +17 JPYtn) ÷ the capacity line (≈ +11.5 JPYtn) = **1.48×**. And from the fitted slope $b \approx -0.65$ to $-0.72$ (chart read, ±1.5 JPYtn per endpoint): a **−10%** year in FX-and-bond returns generates **+6.5–7.2 JPYtn ≈ \$41–45bn** of JGB buying automatically — **54–60% of the entire "policy lever", with no policy.**
7. **The note's own flow mechanism has already flipped sign — 13 days after publication.** USD/JPY **162.512 (20-Jul, publication) → 157.400 (2-Aug) = −3.15%**, a yen *rally* (verified from `USDJPY=X`). Foreign sleeves ≈ 48–50% of AUM, so foreign assets lose ~3.15% in JPY, AUM falls to ≈ \$2.758tn, and the domestic-bond share rises from 27.00% to **27.41–27.43% — +0.41–0.43ppt for free, in ten lake sessions** (2026-07-20 → 2026-08-02; nine business days plus the Sunday 2-Aug FX print). That is **~10–11% of the entire 4ppt discretionary room consumed by FX alone**, shrinking mechanical headroom from \$112bn to **≈\$99bn**. Push further and the sign inverts: continued yen strength drifts the share toward the 31% ceiling and turns GPIF into a **JGB seller**. The note assumes the weak-yen state; the state changed almost immediately.
8. **NISA sized properly — not a market event.** Growth quota = 0.75 × JPY71tn = **JPY53.25tn**. At the note's own observed 4% household direct-JGB weight: **JPY2.13tn ≈ \$13bn** — **18%** of the GPIF lever, **0.21ppt** of free float, **0.21–0.83bp** on 30y spreads under its own β range. Even a wildly aggressive 10% allocation gives JPY5.33tn ≈ \$33bn (44% of the GPIF lever).
