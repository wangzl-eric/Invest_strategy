## 1. Executive summary

Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research published this *Global Markets Analyst* on 21 May 2026 to answer the question the Warsh nomination put back on the table for institutional rates clients: whether the Fed's balance sheet is about to shrink, and by how much it *could* shrink without breaking dollar funding. The method is an accounting identity plus one regression — the identity sets the asset growth needed to keep reserves stable as a share of bank assets given trend growth in currency, the TGA and the banking system, while an OLS fit of the TGCR−IORB spread on reserves+RRP as a share of bank assets, T-bills as a share of money-fund balances, negative reserve shocks and a quarter-end dummy pins the reserve share consistent with repo setting slightly below IORB, with three static "headroom" estimates layered on top of today's levels of bank assets, currency and a \$900bn TGA target. GS argues — in its own hedged words, that year-to-date evidence "supports the argument that **distributional considerations contributed to** late-2025 repo volatility", not that they were the whole of it — that the re-coupling of repo with its fitted value points away from an aggregate reserve shortage, and therefore expects reserve-management purchases to slow to **\$5bn/mo from June 2026 through 1Q27**, taking reserves to **11% of bank assets**, before a **\$25bn/mo (\$300bn/yr)** trend pace holds them there — with Fed ownership of Treasuries rising only ~1pp to **15.3%** by end-decade, still below the ~17% pre-GFC average. The report concedes real downside risk — a merely *stable* balance sheet erodes reserves **\$150bn/yr and 1.2pp/yr of share**, reaching the 2019 lows by end-decade, and three routes (repo above IORB, LCR/ILST credit for pre-pledged liquidity, TGA sterilization) sum to roughly **\$1tn, or 4% of bank assets** — but concludes that the bar to a meaningful outright reduction is high. Confidence is asserted, not measured: the three headroom figures and the \$1tn aggregate are point estimates carrying one verbal hedge ("uncertainty bands around those estimates are wide"), and the two load-bearing regressions report **no $R^2$, no standard errors, no sample size**.

## 2. Methodology & reasoning

Five pillars carry the note. Pillar 1 is the empirical claim everything else stands on; Pillar 2 is the accounting spine; Pillar 3 is the headroom construct that carries the "downside risks are real" conclusion; Pillar 4 defuses the market-footprint objection; Pillar 5 is the policy rebuttal to Warsh, and it is the one paragraph with no number in it.

### Pillar 1 — GS's claim: the 2025 repo blowup was distributional, not an aggregate shortage

- **Point:** The single load-bearing empirical claim. Everything downstream — \$225bn / \$475bn / \$1tn of headroom, the \$5bn/mo RMP path — collapses without it. GS's own formulation is deliberately weaker than the use it is later put to: *"We think year-to-date evidence supports the argument that distributional considerations **contributed to** late-2025 repo volatility."*
- **Evidence:** Exhibit 11 shows TGCR−IORB setting well above its fitted OLS value through late 2025, then **re-coupling in 2026 with rates both lower and more stable through April, despite reserves falling to a cycle low relative to bank assets.** Named trigger: the TGA rose **~\$700bn over 3–4 months** after the debt-limit resolution. My independent pull of the exact series: TGCR−IORB monthly means **2025Q4 +3.69bp (max +25bp) → 2026Q1 −1.56 → Apr −2.90 → May −7.85 → Jun −4.29 → Jul −5.38**, latest **−2.0bp** (30-Jul-2026). TGCR 99th-pct − IORB: **2025Q4 max +38bp → July-2026 mean −0.9bp**, latest +1.0bp.
- **The decisive number the report does not print:** the reserve share at the October-2025 blowup was **11.65%** (29-Oct-2025, reserves \$2,848bn). Today's is **11.625% — lower — with TGCR−IORB at −2.0bp.**
- **Chain:** Repo set 25bp above IORB in late 2025 while the fitted model said it should not have → an aggregate shortage would persist until reserve *supply* changed → instead the deviation closed to zero and went negative while supply kept falling (11.65% → 11.625%) → GS reads the residual as **friction in the distribution of reserves across banks rather than scarcity in aggregate** → frictions heal with time and price incentives, scarcity does not → **the Fed can let reserves keep falling as a share of bank assets without re-triggering stress, so \$5bn/mo of growth suffices for now.**
- **Read:** Inferential and the most load-bearing claim in the note. The evidence is genuinely strong — my pull confirms re-coupling at a reserve share *below* the crisis level, a harder test than the one GS ran. **Where it leaps:** the note concedes in a single sentence that *"a portion of the year-to-date easing in funding conditions [may reflect] a shift in reserve demand due to increased willingness to use the [standing repo] facility"* after the mid-December-2025 removal of the aggregate operation limit and the dropped "backstop" label (Exhibit 14, split at 15-Dec-2025) — then quantifies headroom as if that confound were resolved. If the SRP redesign is doing the work, the easing is a facility subsidy, the Exhibit 11 residual is measuring the Fed's own backstop rather than bank behaviour, and every headroom figure becomes a measure of how far the Fed can lean on an untested facility. **Live data cannot separate the two:** the collapse in TGCR99−IORB from +38bp to −0.9bp is what *both* stories predict.

### Pillar 2 — The baseline is expansion: a clean identity that ties to 3% on live data

- **Point:** "\$25bn/mo, \$300bn/yr, 11% steady state" is an accounting identity, not a forecast — and it reconciles component-by-component.
- **Evidence:** Stated assumptions — 4.5% trend nominal growth in currency and bank assets, ~6%/yr TGA growth (Exhibit 9 footnote), \$900bn TGA target. Decomposition: **\$9bn/mo currency + \$11bn/mo bank-asset matching + \$5bn/mo TGA = \$25bn/mo.** Near-term path: RMPs at **\$5bn/mo from June 2026 through 1Q27** (10 months) vs the **\$10bn/mo announced** and **\$40bn/mo initial** pace, taking reserves from the "11.5–12% seen recently" to **11%**. Under a stable balance sheet: **−\$150bn/yr and −1.2pp/yr, ~4pp by end-decade, to the 2019 lows**; at \$20bn/mo of runoff, **−7pp (2pp/yr)**.
- **Chain:** Holding $\rho$ constant requires assets to grow enough to cover three independently growing non-reserve claims plus reserve growth itself → currency grows with nominal GDP (~4.5%), the TGA with peak weekly Treasury withdrawals (~6%), reserves with bank assets (4.5% × 11%) → summing on live inputs gives **\$24.2bn/mo**, which GS rounds to \$25bn → anything less is contractionary in reserve terms even if the balance sheet is flat or growing → \$5bn/mo therefore drains reserves at ~\$9bn/mo → **the share slides from ~11.9% to 11% by 1Q27 by design, then \$25bn/mo holds it there.**
- **Read:** Descriptive accounting, load-bearing on the *pace* not the direction, and the most reproducible part of the note. On live FRED inputs the identity gives **\$24.2bn/mo vs the printed \$25bn (3.2% gap)**, component-by-component **\$9.06 / \$10.59 / \$4.55bn/mo vs \$9 / \$11 / \$5**. Every input GS left implicit is recoverable and correct. **Two leaps.** (i) It presents $\rho^{*}=11\%$ as if it drives the run rate; it barely does — at $\rho^{*}=10\%$ the steady-state pace only falls to ~\$24bn/mo, because the "how much to shrink" debate is a one-time **\$250–475bn *level* question, not a flow question**, and the framing obscures that. (ii) The **\$5bn/mo forecast is not the announced policy** (\$10bn/mo) — it is GS's judgement that the Fed slows further, and live `WALCL` has run at **+\$10.67bn/mo since publication, i.e. at the announced pace, 2.1× GS's forecast.**
- *Drafting artifact worth one line:* Exhibit 9's title reads "\$20bn per month in trend balance sheet **growth**" while its own legend and the body text describe \$20bn/mo of **runoff** against a \$25bn/mo baseline — a stale headline, not an analytical error, but it is the kind of thing to check before quoting an exhibit title.

### Pillar 3 — The ~\$1tn / 4%-of-bank-assets headroom, and why it is the weakest construct in the note

- **Point:** This is the number that carries the "downside risks are real" conclusion.
- **Evidence:** Three routes summed:
  - **(a) \$475bn** — allow funding costs to settle **1–2bp *above*** IORB rather than 1–2bp below, i.e. reserves at **10%** rather than 11% of bank assets. Achievable via a stable balance sheet through YE27 or \$20bn/mo of shrinkage through 1Q27.
  - **(b) \$375bn** — willingness to return to pre-GFC reliance on contingent liquidity, peak daylight overdrafts averaging **~1.5% of bank assets** (Exhibit 13), enabled by LCR/ILST credit for pre-pledged discount-window and FHLB capacity worth up to **29pp of LCR upside across the top 7 banks** (assuming 50% of estimated pre-pledged DW capacity included) and ~**\$600bn** of surplus HQLA.
  - **(c) \$200bn** — a buffer against a **95th-percentile one-month TGA-driven reserve drop**.
  - Sum = **\$1,050bn ≈ 4% of bank assets**; base-case headroom (to 11%) is **\$225bn**. The note's only hedge: *"uncertainty bands around those estimates are wide."*
- **Chain:** Three distinct frictions each hold reserve demand above its technical minimum → relieving each frees a measurable quantity → \$475bn + \$375bn + \$200bn ≈ \$1tn ≈ 4% of bank assets → **a meaningfully smaller balance sheet is technically feasible, so a period of balance-sheet stability is plausible.**
- **Read:** Inferential and explicitly corroborating — the note refuses to bank it, and the *summation* is where it leaps hardest.
  - The three are added **as if independent**, but **(a) is a level** (the drop from ~11.9% to 10% of bank assets) while **(b) is a shift in the demand curve** that would move where that 10% point sits. Double-counting is at minimum the smaller of the two, **\$375bn**.
  - **(c) is mis-calibrated on a calm sample.** Live 4-week TGA increases: **p95 = +\$290.1bn (2019–present), p99 = +\$413.7bn, max = +\$568.2bn.** GS's \$200bn matches the **trailing-12-month** p95 (\$211.1bn), not the full post-2019 p95 — so the buffer is sized to *miss* the post-debt-limit rebuild the note itself names as the trigger of the stress it is analysing. Correcting to the full-sample p95 lifts the buffer to **\$290bn** — and the *sign* of that correction depends entirely on how route (c) is read, which the note never makes explicit. Read GS's way — the buffer is reserves the Fed can stop holding once TGA swings are sterilized — a bigger buffer is a bigger credit and the aggregate *rises* to **\$1,140bn**. Read the other way — the buffer is a floor that has to survive a real shock, and Treasury's cash-lending fix "seems likely to take time" — the extra \$90bn is not a credit at all but an addition to the reserve floor, cutting the usable aggregate to **\$960bn**. GS books it as a credit while sizing it on the calm sample, which is the only combination in which \$200bn is both fully available *and* adequate.
  - **And the destination is free.** \$1,050bn ÷ \$256.7bn/pp = **4.09pp**, taking reserves from 11.90% to **7.81%** — essentially the 2019 trough of **7.99%**, which organic erosion under a merely stable balance sheet reaches in **3.37 years of doing nothing**. The whole activist agenda is worth ~3.4 years of patience.
  - **Sensitivity of the headline:** the \$225bn base case is entirely a function of where you read $\rho_0$ inside GS's own "11.5–12%" range — holding GS's own implied \$250bn/pp base fixed so only $\rho_0$ moves, **\$125bn at 11.5%, \$250bn at 12.0%**, a **−44% / +11% band from a single stated input**, before any modelling error.

### Pillar 4 — The Fed's Treasury footprint stays below its pre-GFC norm even while it buys

- **Point:** The argument that defuses the "Fed dominance" objection to an expanding balance sheet.
- **Evidence:** Fed UST holdings **~14% of USTs outstanding** today vs a **~17% pre-GFC average** (1954–2008, Exhibit 2). Agency MBS is the real footprint shift: **0% pre-2008, ~22% of the market today** (Exhibit 3), with a stated Treasury-only goal and current policy of reinvesting MBS runoff into T-bills. Baseline UST purchases **\$20–25bn/mo near term, \$35–40bn/mo steady state**. End-decade scenarios (Exhibit 10): baseline **15.3%** (+~1pp); stable balance sheet with MBS reinvestment **~13%**; MBS runoff without reinvestment **11.3%** (−3pp). Purchases stay **entirely in T-bills until at least 2028**, by which point the bill share of Fed UST holdings (~25%) is roughly market-neutral.
- **Chain:** Deficits grow the marketable Treasury stock faster than the Fed grows its book → even at \$35–40bn/mo the Fed's share rises only ~1.3pp over ~3.5 years → 15.3% stays 1.7pp below the 1954–2008 average → **buying USTs at the steady-state pace is running to stand still, not dominance, so the footprint objection does not bind.**
- **Read:** Descriptive on the historical shares, **forecast-dependent on the projection — and the reassurance is a deficit forecast in disguise.** My reconstruction: live `TREAST` \$4,519.7bn at "about 14%" ⟹ market ≈ **\$32.3tn**; baseline buying to end-2029 = 10 × \$22.5bn + 32 × \$37.5bn = **\$1,425bn** ⟹ holdings \$5,945bn; for that to be 15.3% the market must reach **\$38.9tn, i.e. ~\$1.9tn/yr of net issuance.** At **\$1.4tn/yr** instead, the market reaches only \$37.2tn and the Fed's share is **16.0%, not 15.3%**, narrowing the "1.7pp below pre-GFC" cushion to **1.0pp**. Cross-checks tie: stable-balance-sheet scenario reconstructs to **13.1%** vs GS's "near 13%"; UST-holdings-unchanged to **11.6%** vs GS's 11.3%. Second unstated point: the note calls the return to a Treasury-only portfolio "gradual" without dating it — at the observed **\$15.6–20.4bn/mo of paydowns on a \$1,930.9bn book (9.7–12.7%/yr)**, that is an **8–10 year project running to ~2034–2036.**

### Pillar 5 — Warsh's premise rejected, but only in words

- **Point:** The claim that a smaller balance sheet creates room to lower the funds rate is dismissed in the final paragraph — and not one number is attached.
- **Evidence:** The note's argument: a higher **free float** (Treasuries held by non-Fed buyers) "would typically bias longer-term yields higher and push swap spreads tighter"; regulatory changes that increase intermediation capacity (eSLR changes, the proposed GSIB buffer recalibration) can mitigate; and Treasury, if it accounts for the equilibrium Fed balance sheet when setting issuance composition, would offset by issuing more bills to the public. Warsh's position: opposed QE2, resigned after it was announced, called for a smaller balance sheet in the lead-up to his selection and at his confirmation hearing. Fed assets are **~21% of GDP** today (6.4% mid-2008, ~36% peak 2022), having grown **\$5.8tn since June 2008**.
- **Chain:** Shrinking transfers duration from the Fed to private holders → free float rises → term premium rises and swap spreads tighten → **long-end financial conditions tighten even as the front end eases**, so the funds-rate "room" is not free → unless regulation raises intermediation capacity or Treasury shifts issuance to bills → **the case for shrinking cannot rest on "it lets us cut," so the debate must be fought on reserve-demand grounds.**
- **Read:** Inferential, presented as corroborating, but strategically the most important paragraph in the note — and the one place where the absence of a number is conspicuous, **because the arithmetic is easy and cuts sharply in GS's favour**: **+6 to +16bp of 10y term premium against a live 45bp 2s10s, versus ~\$0.4bn/yr of consolidated saving on a bill-backed book** (see arithmetic #6 and #7 below). **Two leaps:** (i) the offsetting Treasury-issuance channel assumes Treasury targets *public-held* duration, which TBAC has never formally committed to and which the note's own TGA discussion shows Treasury moves slowly on ("operational and implementation considerations" still under study); (ii) "regulatory changes can mitigate" carries heavy weight with no magnitude. *Not* double-counting in the strict sense — the note keeps the two channels separate, LCR/ILST credit for pre-pledged collateral driving the \$375bn of reserve-demand reduction in Pillar 3, and eSLR / GSIB-buffer recalibration driving intermediation capacity here — **but both are the same bet that regulatory reform arrives and works, and that bet is charged as a hurdle in Pillar 3 ("a formal proposal and rulemaking process") while being taken as free in Pillar 5.**

### The arithmetic the note leaves on the table

Every figure below was computed on 2026-08-03 from independently pulled FRED data, except where a parameter is explicitly taken from outside the report (the term-premium elasticity $\kappa$ in #6). These are the calculations that decide whether the note's conclusion holds, and the report runs none of them.

**1. Inverting GS's own two headroom figures recovers their unstated inputs — and live data has eaten a quarter of the room.** $H(11\%)=\$225$bn and $H(10\%)=\$475$bn are two equations in two unknowns: subtracting gives $0.01B = \$250$bn ⟹ **$B=\$25.0$tn and $\rho_0 = 11.90\%$**, neither of which GS prints. Live: $B=\$25{,}673$bn, $\rho_0 = 11.625\%$ ⟹ $H(11\%)=\$160$bn (**GS 41% too high**) and $H(10\%)=\$417$bn (14% too high). **Ten weeks after publication, \$64bn — 29% — of the base-case headroom is already gone.**

**2. The steady-state identity ties component-by-component.** Currency \$2,415.7bn × 4.5% ÷ 12 = **\$9.06bn/mo** (GS \$9) ✓; bank-asset matching \$25,673.4bn × 4.5% × 11% ÷ 12 = **\$10.59bn/mo** (GS \$11) ✓; TGA \$910.8bn × 6% ÷ 12 = **\$4.55bn/mo** (GS \$5) ✓. Total **\$24.2bn/mo = \$290.4bn/yr** vs GS's \$25bn/mo = \$300bn/yr — **3.2%**.

**3. The drift identity ties, and reveals a \$13bn/yr rounding in GS's favour.** $d\rho/dt = -0.636 - 0.523 = -1.16$pp/yr vs GS's 1.2pp/yr ✓. But the absolute drain is $g_C C + g_T\mathrm{TGA} = 108.7 + 54.6 = \$163.3$bn/yr while GS prints **\$150bn/yr — 8% light**, a gap consistent with netting ~\$13bn/yr of declining foreign RRP / other liabilities (live `WLRRAL` \$337.1bn) that is never stated.

**4. "About 4pp, back to the 2019 lows, by end of the decade" reconciles exactly.** Live 2019 trough: **7.99% on 18-Sep-2019** (reserves \$1,394bn / bank assets \$17,451bn). From $\rho_0 = 11.90\%$ at 1.16pp/yr, 7.99% arrives in **3.37 years ≈ September 2029**. Both "about 4pp" (11.90 − 7.99 = 3.91pp) and "end of the decade" tie. This is the note's cleanest internal reconciliation and it survives independent data.

**5. The \$20bn/mo runoff scenario, priced out — the report never does this.** 7pp from 11.90% ⟹ **4.90% of bank assets**. Bank assets at end-2029 = \$25,673 × 1.045^3.4 = \$29,825bn ⟹ reserves = **\$1,462bn** — only **\$68bn (+4.9%) above the absolute September-2019 repo-crisis level of \$1,394bn, on a banking system 71% larger**, and **3.09pp below the share at which repo broke in 2019.** GS presents this as a scenario; it is a deliberate overshoot past a known breaking point, and the closing warning about "the potential costs of overshooting" never attaches this number to it.

**6. Warsh's premise, priced.** Fed assets \$6.738tn at "about 21% of GDP" ⟹ NGDP ≈ **\$32.1tn**; a \$1tn shrink = **3.11% of GDP**. At the conventional **2–5bp of 10y term premium per 1% of GDP** of balance sheet — *the literature's range, not GS's: the note attaches no number at all to this channel, and the band below is mine* — that is **+6 to +16bp on the 10y** — against a live 2s10s of **45bp**, i.e. **13–36% of the entire current curve slope**, in exchange for an amount of front-end "room" Warsh has never quantified.

**7. The fiscal case for shrinking is \$0.4bn/yr; the case against is 220× larger.** Purchases are "entirely in T-Bills until at least 2028." Live `DTB3` 3.69% vs `IORB` 3.65% ⟹ the Fed earns **4bp** over its funding cost on bill-backed reserves, so shrinking \$1tn saves the consolidated government **\$0.4bn/yr** — about 0.02% of a ~\$1.9tn deficit. Currency seigniorage is \$2,415.7bn × 3.65% = **\$88.2bn/yr** — and that is a *floor*, because `CURRCIR` last observes 2025-10-01, so the stock is ~10 months out of date. The note calls currency "a net positive from a fiscal perspective" and sizes neither side.

**8. Dollars per basis point of funding pressure — the most useful trading number in the note, and it is absent.** At GS's implied slope, 1bp of TGCR−IORB = 0.333pp of bank assets = **\$85.6bn of reserves**; at the *realized* slope (#9, $\beta_1 \approx -13.6$ bp/pp), only **\$18.9bn**. So the entire near-term drain — \$5bn/mo of RMPs against a \$25bn/mo neutral pace for 10 months = **\$200bn of foregone supply** — is worth **2.3bp on GS's slope but ~10.6bp on the realized one**. **The 1Q27 glide path is a 2bp non-event only if GS's slope is right; on the realized data it is a 10bp move.**

**9. The realized repo/reserve slope is several times steeper than GS's assumed slope — but it is not identified at this sample size.** GS's anchors imply $\beta_1 = -3.0$ bp/pp. *Every* internally consistent pairing of realized 2026 data is steeper, and by a lot:

| Pairing | $\Delta s$ | $\Delta\rho$ | $\beta_1^{\text{realized}}$ | Implied $s$ at $\rho=10\%$ |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Monthly means, May→Jul-26 (−7.85→−5.38bp on 12.105%→11.892%) | +2.47bp | −0.213pp | **−11.6** bp/pp | ≈ +17bp |
| OLS on four monthly means, Apr–Jul-26 ($R^2 = 0.50$, $n=4$) | — | — | **−13.6** bp/pp | ≈ +21bp |
| Weekly endpoints, 20-May (−15bp @ 12.276%) → 29-Jul (−1bp @ 11.625%) | +14bp | −0.651pp | **−21.5** bp/pp | ≈ +34bp |

*Honest caveat: $n \le 4$, TGA-rebuild-driven, $R^2 = 0.50$, no error band, and none of these is GS's actual regressor (reserves **+ RRP** at the weekly minimum, not reserves alone).* The point estimates are unusable individually and the spread between them is the finding. What survives is the sign and the order of magnitude: **nothing in the realized data supports a slope as flat as −3.0 bp/pp, so a \$475bn drain to 10% is far more likely to overshoot GS's +1-to-2bp target than to land on it.**

### Load-bearing formulas

**Steady-state balance-sheet growth (the \$25bn/mo):**

$$\Delta A \;=\; g_C\,C \;+\; g_T\,\mathrm{TGA} \;+\; \rho^{*}\,g_B\,B$$

with $A$ = Fed total assets, $C$ = currency in circulation, $\mathrm{TGA}$ = Treasury General Account, $B$ = US commercial bank assets, $\rho^{*}$ = target reserves/bank-assets ratio, $g_C, g_T, g_B$ = trend growth of currency, TGA, bank assets. Live inputs: $0.045(2{,}415.7) + 0.06(910.8) + 0.11(0.045)(25{,}673.4) = 108.7 + 54.6 + 127.1 = \$290.4$bn/yr $= \$24.2$bn/mo (GS prints \$25bn/mo).

**Reserve-share drift under a fixed balance sheet** ($\Delta A = 0$), i.e. why "stable" is contractionary:

$$\frac{d\rho}{dt} \;=\; \frac{\dot R}{B} - \rho\,\frac{\dot B}{B} \;=\; -\,\frac{g_C C + g_T \mathrm{TGA}}{B} \;-\; \rho\, g_B$$

with $\rho = R/B$, $R$ = reserve balances. Live: $-0.00636 - 0.00523 = -1.16$pp/yr (GS: 1.2pp/yr); absolute drain $\dot R = -\$163.3$bn/yr (GS: ≈ −\$150bn/yr).

**Static headroom** — the construct behind every "\$Xbn of room" figure:

$$H(\rho^{*}) \;=\; (\rho_0 - \rho^{*})\,B$$

Inverting GS's pair $H(11\%)=\$225$bn, $H(10\%)=\$475$bn ⟹ $B = \$250$bn/pp $= \$25.0$tn and $\rho_0 = 11.90\%$. Live: $B = \$25{,}673$bn (\$256.7bn/pp), $\rho_0 = 11.625\%$ ⟹ $H(11\%) = \$160$bn, $H(10\%) = \$417$bn.

**The Exhibit 8/11 repo pricing curve** (the note's only regression, reported with no diagnostics):

$$s \;\equiv\; \mathrm{TGCR} - \mathrm{IORB} \;=\; \beta_0 + \beta_1\rho + \beta_2\,\mathrm{bills}^{\mathrm{MMF}} + \beta_3\,\mathrm{shock}^{-} + \beta_4 D_{qe} + \varepsilon$$

with $s$ in bp; $\rho = (\text{reserves} + \text{RRP})/B$ at the **weekly minimum**; $\mathrm{bills}^{\mathrm{MMF}}$ = T-bills as a share of money-fund balances; $\mathrm{shock}^{-}$ = negative reserve shock (fitted lines use the trailing-2y average and the 95th percentile); $D_{qe}$ = quarter-end dummy. GS's own anchors imply $\beta_1 = \frac{(+1.5)-(-1.5)}{10\% - 11\%} = -3.0$ bp/pp; OLS on Apr–Jul-2026 monthly means gives $-13.6$ bp/pp ($R^2 = 0.50$, $n = 4$; see #9 for the full range, $-11.6$ to $-21.5$).

**Dollar value of a basis point of funding pressure** (never printed in the note):

$$\frac{\partial R}{\partial s} \;=\; \frac{B/100}{|\beta_1|} \;=\; \frac{\$256.7\text{bn/pp}}{3.0\ \text{bp/pp}} = \$85.6\text{bn per bp}\qquad\left(\text{realized slope }13.6:\ \$18.9\text{bn per bp}\right)$$

**Breakeven tail probability for the implied long TGCR−IORB position:**

$$p^{*} \;=\; \frac{c}{\Pi_{\text{tail}} + c}, \qquad c = 1\ \text{bp/quarter carry},\; \Pi_{\text{tail}} = 27\ \text{bp} \;\Longrightarrow\; p^{*} = 3.6\%\ \text{per quarter}$$

against an empirical base rate of $2/28 = 7.1\%$ per quarter.

**Term-premium cost of shrinking (the un-run Warsh calculation):**

$$\Delta TP_{10y} \;\approx\; \kappa\,\frac{|\Delta A|}{\mathrm{NGDP}}, \qquad \kappa \in [2,5]\ \text{bp per 1\% of GDP}$$

With $|\Delta A| = \$1$tn and $\mathrm{NGDP} = \$6.738\text{tn}/0.21 = \$32.1$tn: $\Delta TP_{10y} \in [+6, +16]$ bp, i.e. **13–36% of the live 45bp 2s10s slope**.

**Consolidated-government cost of carrying the balance sheet:**

$$\Pi_{\text{fiscal}} \;=\; A\,(y_A - i_{\mathrm{IORB}}) \;+\; C\,i_{\mathrm{IORB}}$$

For a bill-backed book, $y_A - i = 3.69\% - 3.65\% = 4$bp ⟹ shrinking \$1tn saves **\$0.4bn/yr**; currency seigniorage $= \$2{,}415.7\text{bn} \times 3.65\% = \$88.2$bn/yr.

**Weakest link (across all five pillars):** **the entire "room to shrink" case rests on one identification claim the report itself flags as unresolved and then treats as settled — that 2026's funding-market easing reveals lower *reserve demand* rather than the Fed's own December-2025 SRP redesign doing the work.**

- The chain is: repo re-coupled with its fitted value while reserves fell to a cycle low → the late-2025 stress was distributional → reserves can safely fall further → **\$225bn / \$475bn / \$375bn / \$200bn / ~\$1tn of headroom.** Every number hangs off the first link.
- But the SRP section concedes: *"it is possible that a portion of the year-to-date easing in funding conditions reflects a shift in reserve demand due to increased willingness to use the facility"* — after the mid-December-2025 removal of the aggregate operation limit and the dropped "backstop" label, the change Exhibit 14 is explicitly dated around. **That sentence is a confession that the identification is unresolved. The note never returns to it.**
- The distinction is the whole trade. **If reserve demand genuinely fell**, the \$1tn is real bank behaviour and the Fed can drain into it. **If the SRP redesign is doing the work**, the easing is a facility subsidy, the Exhibit 11 residual measures the Fed's own backstop rather than banks' liquidity preferences, and "\$1tn of headroom" is really an estimate of how far the Fed can lean on a facility never tested at scale, in stress, under a balance-sheet-hostile Chair.
