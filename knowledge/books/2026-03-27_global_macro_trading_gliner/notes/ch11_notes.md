# GMT Chapter 11 Notes: The Role of Central Banks in Global Macro

## Core Concept and Author Intent

Chapter 11 presents central banks as the key institutions that translate macro conditions into market prices. Gliner's aim is to make the reader fluent in policy objectives, implementation tools, constraints, and communication. The chapter's main practical message is that global macro traders are not just trading data; they are trading the reaction function of policy makers.

## Core Mental Models

- Central banks shape the price and quantity of liquidity.
- Price stability is the anchor objective even when growth and employment are also policy goals.
- Policy tools affect markets through reserves, expectations, and exchange rates.
- Communication is itself a policy tool.
- The zero lower bound changes the toolkit and pushes central banks toward balance-sheet policies.
- A country cannot simultaneously have a fixed exchange rate, free capital flows, and independent monetary policy.

## Practitioner Heuristics

- Know each central bank's formal mandate before interpreting its actions.
- Distinguish policy-rate moves from balance-sheet moves; they are not interchangeable.
- Watch reserve requirements and FX intervention in countries where those tools matter more than policy rates.
- Treat minutes, statements, and speaker tone as inputs to the policy reaction function.
- Pay special attention when a hawk sounds dovish or a dove sounds hawkish; the signal content is asymmetric.
- In ZLB regimes, track asset purchases, forward guidance, and reserve remuneration rather than only the headline policy rate.

## Key Frameworks

### 1. Central-Bank Objective Framework

The chapter emphasizes that major central banks have explicit or implicit objectives around:

- price stability
- employment or growth smoothing
- financial stability

The exact mandate differs across the Fed, ECB, BOE, and SNB, but the practical lesson is the same: the trader should map policy decisions back to each institution's formal priorities.

### 2. Policy Toolkit Framework

Gliner organizes the main tools as:

- reserve ratios
- policy rates
- open market operations and repos
- discount window lending
- currency intervention
- Taylor-rule style policy guidance

The chapter is effectively showing the reader how central banks change both the availability and the price of liquidity.

### 3. Impossible Trinity

The impossible trinity is one of the chapter's clearest conceptual frameworks:

- fixed exchange rate
- free capital flows
- independent monetary policy

You can choose only two. This is a useful macro diagnostic because it explains why some countries keep sacrificing exchange-rate flexibility while others sacrifice policy autonomy.

### 4. Money and Reserve Framework

The chapter separates:

- monetary base
- money supply
- reserves
- foreign reserves

That matters because balance-sheet growth, reserve accumulation, and money growth are related but not identical. Traders often conflate them.

### 5. ZLB and QE Framework

Once rates hit the zero lower bound, the policy regime changes. The chapter highlights:

- liquidity-trap risk
- quantitative easing
- operation twist / maturity transformation
- diminishing efficacy of repeated asset purchases

The practical lesson is that in ZLB regimes, central banks rely much more on expectations management and balance-sheet engineering than on conventional rate moves.

### 6. Communication Framework

The communication section is especially useful for discretionary macro:

- statements reveal the current narrative
- minutes reveal internal vote dispersion
- hawk/dove mapping reveals speaker bias

This is one of the best practitioner sections in the chapter because it treats language as tradable information rather than background color.
