# GMT Chapter 6 Notes: Systematic Trading

## Core Concept and Author Intent

Chapter 6 gives a high-level blueprint for turning macro investing into a rule-based process. Gliner's main claim is that systematic trading is not just about automation; it is about decomposing macro investing into assets, strategy sleeves, factors, and risk overlays so that positions are generated by repeatable rules rather than emotion. The chapter is broad rather than mathematically deep, but it is valuable because it lays out the factor vocabulary the author thinks systematic macro should be built from.

## Core Mental Models

- Systematic macro is a decomposition problem: asset group -> strategy sleeve -> factors -> risk overlays -> portfolio.
- Removing emotion is useful, but the real advantage is consistent measurement and sizing.
- Value, trend, carry, and fundamentals are reusable building blocks across asset classes.
- Risk factors should modify a signal before it becomes a position.
- Risk premia are attractive because they are simple and scalable, but they often hide crash risk.
- Risk parity is about balancing risk contributions, not capital weights.

## Practitioner Heuristics

- Define the asset universe first; do not start with factors in the abstract.
- Backtest each new factor alone before combining it with the stack.
- Prefer factors with strong standalone Sharpe and acceptable drawdown behavior.
- Increase confidence when multiple sleeves agree, especially when value and trend align.
- Always model carry, because financing and roll shape long-run realized returns.
- Overlay liquidity, inflation, and tail-risk constraints before accepting raw model output.
- Treat static allocations with suspicion; rebalance by risk when possible.
- Assume crowded risk-premia trades can gap against you harder than simple backtests suggest.

## Key Frameworks

### 1. Systematic Macro Construction Stack

The chapter's core framework is:

1. choose asset groups
2. assign strategy sleeves
3. choose factors within each sleeve
4. apply risk-factor overlays
5. aggregate into a portfolio

This is the most important reusable model in the chapter.

### 2. Four Strategy Sleeves

Gliner emphasizes:

- value
- trend
- carry
- fundamentals

These sleeves are intentionally broad so they can be reused across FX, equities, fixed income, and commodities.

### 3. Factor Design Logic

Examples the chapter gives include:

- equities value via `P/E`, `price/book`, and free-cash-flow yield
- FX value via purchasing-power parity and terms of trade
- commodity carry via curve shape
- macro fundamentals via growth, inflation, unemployment, CDS, and related indicators

The important methodological point is that a sleeve should be the weighted combination of explicit factors, not a vague concept.

### 4. Risk-Factor Overlay

Signals are not enough. Gliner wants the model to adjust for:

- inflation risk
- liquidity and transaction-cost risk
- tail-event vulnerability
- market-specific constraints

This is a strong practitioner idea because many backtests fail at the overlay layer rather than the signal layer.

### 5. Risk Premia Map

The chapter treats several strategies as compensation for bearing uncomfortable risk:

- FX carry
- equity risk premium
- volatility-selling type premia
- fixed-income rolldown
- commodity curve carry

The key lesson is that positive expected return often comes bundled with episodic but severe drawdowns.

### 6. Risk Parity Framework

Gliner uses risk parity to make a simple but important point:

- a capital allocation is not the same thing as a risk allocation
- classic `60/40` is far more equity-risk-heavy than it appears
- dynamic volatility targeting and broader diversification can improve the portfolio

This is a portfolio-construction extension of the rest of the chapter's factor logic.

## What Seems Immediately Testable with Available Data

- Cross-asset models using value, trend, carry, and macro-fundamental sleeves on liquid futures or ETF proxies.
- Factor-level ablation tests to see which sleeves actually add incremental Sharpe.
- Commodity carry signals based on curve shape and seasonality.
- FX carry and simple PPP-style value measures.
- Risk-parity portfolios with and without volatility targeting.
- Drawdown sensitivity of risk-premia strategies during `1987`, `1998`, `2008`, `2020`, and other crisis windows.

## What Likely Requires External or Harder-to-Source Data

- Institutional transaction-cost and liquidity estimates for harder-to-trade markets.
- Clean cross-country macro fundamentals with consistent release timing for signal construction.
- Full futures-curve histories and financing assumptions for better carry estimation.
- Crowd-ownership data for risk-premia capacity analysis.

## Material Score

- Credibility: 4/5. The factor taxonomy is standard and useful, though the chapter stays conceptual and omits many implementation details.
- Relevance: 5/5. This chapter is directly relevant for turning macro ideas into a research pipeline.
- Actionability: 5/5. The proposed sleeves and overlays are straightforward to prototype and audit.

## Open Questions and Things to Verify Empirically

- Which sleeve combinations are actually complementary after accounting for cross-correlation and costs?
- When value and trend disagree, what weighting rule is most robust across asset classes?
- How should liquidity and tail-risk overlays be estimated so they matter before, not after, a crisis?
- Are risk-premia returns decaying as strategies become more crowded?
- Does dynamic risk-parity rebalancing materially outperform simpler fixed-risk budgets out of sample?
